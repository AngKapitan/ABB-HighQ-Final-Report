This thesis presented the comprehensive design, comparative analysis, and experimental validation of a flatwise-wound planar transformer engineered to resolve the fundamental thermal bottlenecks in high-power-density Solid State Transformers (SSTs). Traditional planar transformers predominantly utilize an edgewise winding architecture, which inherently traps heat due to the high thermal resistance of the inter-turn dielectric insulation. To overcome this critical limitation, this study proposed a structural shift to a flatwise orientation, establishing a highly conductive parallel thermal path that grants every individual turn direct, orthogonal access to the external cooling boundaries.

The theoretical advantages of this geometric reconfiguration were first evaluated through comprehensive 3D Multi-Physics Finite Element Analysis (FEA). Simulations, evaluated across multiple geometric aspect ratios, indicated that the flatwise orientation inherently provides a superior thermal path by lowering the peak operating temperature under natural air cooling and forced-air cooling compared to the conventional edgewise equivalent. 

To definitively validate these findings, hardware prototypes of both winding configurations were fabricated and subjected to continuous, full-load experimental evaluation in a 1-kW, 28.5-kHz bidirectional CLLC resonant converter. The experimental hardware testing conclusively validated the core hypothesis of this research. Under continuous operation, the edgewise design suffered from severe internal-to-external thermal gradients, resulting in trapped heat and the rapid onset of thermal runaway. In stark contrast, the flatwise design consistently maintained safe operation, achieving significantly lower thermal gradients and lower peak winding temperatures across all tested cooling conditions.

By narrowing the temperature gradients between the internal copper windings and the core exterior, the experimental data proves that the flatwise architecture successfully establishes a fundamentally lower thermal resistance path. This enables highly efficient heat extraction directly from the core window, fundamentally breaking the restrictive dielectric-thermal trade-off. Ultimately, the findings of this study validate the flatwise-wound planar transformer as a structurally superior candidate for compact, high-reliability power converters, unlocking new limits for volumetric miniaturization in next-generation electrical distribution infrastructure.

A few points for recommendation and future work are suggested as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Model Verification:} While the 3D Multi-Physics Finite Element Analysis (FEA) provided a robust theoretical foundation for the thermal advantages of the flatwise orientation, further refinement of the analytical models used is recommended to provide insights into optimization, especially for bigger and higher power applications where FEA would take too much time and be more challenging depending on the problem.
    
    \item \textbf{Parasitics Modelling:} The transformer utilizes rectangular windings. This directly means that parasitic capacitance will be a problem, as rectangular windings mean a large overlapping surface area. Another direct analogy are planar transformers that utilize PCB windings, where the large overlapping surface areas between adjacent copper layers inherently generate significant inter-turn and inter-winding capacitances. Future work should prioritize the development of an accurate high-frequency analytical model to extract these distributed parasitic elements. Understanding and quantifying these capacitive effects is strictly necessary to accurately predict common-mode electromagnetic interference (EMI), mitigate unintended high-frequency ringing, which is especially important when integrating the transformer in high-power, high-frequency, and high-voltage applications.
    
    \item \textbf{Further Scalability Analysis and Experimentation} As this study successfully validated the flatwise architecture on a scaled-down 1-kW prototype, future work must formally evaluate the geometric and thermal scalability of this design toward the ultimate 100-kW, 1000-V target hardware. Furthermore, transitioning to the 1-kV operating regime necessitates strict adherence to international safety and insulation standards (such as IEC 60664 and IEC 62477). Future physical iterations must incorporate comprehensive insulation coordination, explicitly meeting rigorous creepage and clearance distance requirements, to ensure that the structural modifications necessary for high-voltage compliance do not inadvertently compromise the established thermal advantages of the flatwise winding configuration.
\end{itemize}
