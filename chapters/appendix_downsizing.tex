This appendix details the theoretical derivation for the transformer downsizing effect presented in the Introduction, Figure \ref{fig:downsizing}. The volume comparison between a standard 50 Hz line-frequency transformer (LFT) and a high-frequency solid-state transformer (HFT) is evaluated using the fundamental area-product method. This appendix also presents the motivation for the volume scaling through the AP Method presented in Chapter 6.

\subsection*{The Area-Product Volume Equation}
The overall physical volume of a transformer ($Vol_{\mathrm{trans}}$) is proportional to the area product of its core and can be analytically estimated using the following relationship:
$$ Vol_{\mathrm{trans}} = K_{\mathrm{vol}} \left( \frac{P_{\mathrm{t}}}{K_{\mathrm{u}} \cdot B_{\mathrm{m}} \cdot f \cdot K_{\mathrm{f}} \cdot J_{\mathrm{m}}} \right)^{3/4} $$
where the variables are defined as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $K_{\mathrm{vol}}$: Core volume coefficient
    \item $P_{\mathrm{t}}$: Total input and output power
    \item $K_{\mathrm{f}}$: Waveform coefficient
    \item $K_{\mathrm{u}}$: Window utilization factor
    \item $f$: Frequency of excitation
    \item $J_{\mathrm{m}}$: Current density through windings
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Design Parameters and Assumptions}
To isolate the volume reduction strictly associated with the transition to high-frequency operation, the power rating, core material limits, and winding density are held constant between both transformer designs. The assigned parameters are summarized below:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Operating Power ($P_{\mathrm{t}}$):} 100 kVA
    \item \textbf{Peak Flux Density ($B_{\mathrm{m}}$):} 1.0 T
    \item \textbf{Current Density ($J_{\mathrm{m}}$):} 5 A/mm$^2$
    \item \textbf{Window Utilization ($K_{\mathrm{u}}$):} 0.5
\end{itemize}

The critical variables that shift between the two operating states are the excitation frequency and the waveform coefficient. The conventional LFT operates at a grid frequency of $f_{\mathrm{LFT}} = 50 \text{ Hz}$ with a standard AC sine wave excitation ($K_{\mathrm{f,LFT}} = 4.44$). Conversely, the HFT is driven by power electronic converters utilizing square wave excitation ($K_{\mathrm{f,HFT}} = 4.0$) across a high-frequency sweep of $f_{\mathrm{HFT}} = 1 \text{ kHz}$ to $10 \text{ kHz}$. 

\subsection*{Volume Ratio Derivation}
To determine the downsizing effect, a ratio is established between the high-frequency volume ($Vol_{\mathrm{HFT}}$) and the line-frequency volume ($Vol_{\mathrm{LFT}}$). By evaluating both cases using the area-product method, the identical parameters ($K_{\mathrm{vol}}$, $P_{\mathrm{t}}$, $K_{\mathrm{u}}$, $B_{\mathrm{m}}$, and $J_{\mathrm{m}}$) cancel out, yielding:
$$ \begin{aligned}
    \frac{Vol_{\mathrm{HFT}}}{Vol_{\mathrm{LFT}}} &= \frac{ K_{\mathrm{vol}} \left( \frac{P_{\mathrm{t}}}{K_{\mathrm{u}} \cdot B_{\mathrm{m}} \cdot f_{\mathrm{HFT}} \cdot K_{\mathrm{f,HFT}} \cdot J_{\mathrm{m}}} \right)^{3/4} }{ K_{\mathrm{vol}} \left( \frac{P_{\mathrm{t}}}{K_{\mathrm{u}} \cdot B_{\mathrm{m}} \cdot f_{\mathrm{LFT}} \cdot K_{\mathrm{f,LFT}} \cdot J_{\mathrm{m}}} \right)^{3/4} } \\
    &= \left( \frac{f_{\mathrm{LFT}} \cdot K_{\mathrm{f,LFT}}}{f_{\mathrm{HFT}} \cdot K_{\mathrm{f,HFT}}} \right)^{3/4}
\end{aligned} $$

Substituting the specific grid and waveform values established in B.2:
$$ \begin{aligned}
    \frac{Vol_{\mathrm{HFT}}}{Vol_{\mathrm{LFT}}} = \left( \frac{55.5}{f_{\mathrm{HFT}}} \right)^{3/4}
\end{aligned} $$

\subsection*{Computational Results}
This simplified governing equation allows for the direct plotting of the theoretical volume reduction curve as a function of the solid-state transformer's switching frequency. Evaluating the function at the extremes of the targeted operating range explicitly confirms the curve:

At $1 \text{ kHz}$ ($1,000 \text{ Hz}$):
$$ \frac{Vol_{\mathrm{HFT}}}{Vol_{\mathrm{LFT}}} = \left( \frac{55.5}{1000} \right)^{3/4} = (0.0555)^{0.75} \approx 0.1146 $$
This demonstrates that transitioning to a $1 \text{ kHz}$ square-wave operation theoretically reduces the physical transformer volume to roughly $11.5\%$ of its $50 \text{ Hz}$ equivalent. 

At $10 \text{ kHz}$ ($10,000 \text{ Hz}$):
$$ \frac{Vol_{\mathrm{HFT}}}{Vol_{\mathrm{LFT}}} = \left( \frac{55.5}{10000} \right)^{3/4} = (0.00555)^{0.75} \approx 0.0204 $$
At the upper limit of $10 \text{ kHz}$, the volume footprint collapses to just $2.0\%$ of the traditional line-frequency design, thoroughly validating the core motivation for integrating high-frequency magnetic components in modern power conversion stages.

\subsection*{Volume Scaling through the Area Product}
A more complete notion of what has been provided previously in this appendix, and what has been discussed in Chapter \ref{chap:AP}.

The $AP$ method provides a fundamental analytical relationship connecting the physical geometry of a transformerspecifically the magnetic core cross-sectional area $A_{\mathrm{c}}$ and the core window area $A_{\mathrm{w}}$ to the electrical and magnetic operating constraints of the power converter \cite{Kimura2015, Colonel2004} . This section provides an appendix outlines the step-by-step mathematical derivation of the $AP$ equation and the three-quarter power volume scaling law ($Vol_{\mathrm{trans}} \propto AP^{3/4}$).

\subsubsection*{Fundamental Derivation of the AP  Equation}
The RMS voltages across the primary winding ($V_{\mathrm{p,rms}}$) and secondary winding ($V_{\mathrm{s,rms}}$) are related to the excitation frequency $f$, number of turns ($N_{\mathrm{p}}, N_{\mathrm{s}}$), core cross-sectional area $A_{\mathrm{c}}$, and maximum magnetic flux density $B_{\mathrm{m}}$ as follows:
\begin{align}
    V_{\mathrm{p,rms}} &= K_{\mathrm{f}} f N_{\mathrm{p}} A_{\mathrm{c}} B_{\mathrm{m}}\label{eq:app_faraday_p} \\
    V_{\mathrm{s,rms}} &= K_{\mathrm{f}} f N_{\mathrm{s}} A_{\mathrm{c}} B_{\mathrm{m}}\label{eq:app_faraday_s}
\end{align}
where $K_{\mathrm{f}}$ is the waveform coefficient determined by the excitation voltage waveform ($K_{\mathrm{f}} = 4.44$ for sinusoidal wave excitation and $K_{\mathrm{f}} = 4.0$ for square-wave excitation with a $50\%$ duty cycle).

Next, the window utilization factor (or space factor) $K_{\mathrm{u}}$ defines the ratio of the total bare conductor cross-sectional area within the core window to the total available physical window area $A_{\mathrm{w}}$:
\begin{equation}
    K_{\mathrm{u}} = \frac{N_{\mathrm{p}} A_{\mathrm{p,coil}} + N_{\mathrm{s}} A_{\mathrm{s,coil}}}{A_{\mathrm{w}}} \label{eq:app_Ku}
\end{equation}
where $A_{\mathrm{p,coil}}$ and $A_{\mathrm{s,coil}}$ are the cross-sectional areas of a single primary and secondary conductor turn, respectively. Assuming an equal RMS current density $J_{\mathrm{m}}$ is maintained across both the primary and secondary windings:
\begin{equation}
    J_{\mathrm{m}} = J_{\mathrm{m,p}} = J_{\mathrm{m,s}} = \frac{I_{\mathrm{p,rms}}}{A_{\mathrm{p,coil}}} = \frac{I_{\mathrm{s,rms}}}{A_{\mathrm{s,coil}}}. \label{eq:app_Jm}
\end{equation}
Substituting $A_{\mathrm{p,coil}}$ and $A_{\mathrm{s,coil}}$ from Eq.~\ref{eq:app_Jm} into Eq.~\ref{eq:app_Ku} yields the window area constraint in terms of winding currents:
\begin{equation}
    K_{\mathrm{u}} A_{\mathrm{w}} = N_{\mathrm{p}} \left( \frac{I_{\mathrm{p,rms}}}{J_{\mathrm{m}}} \right) + N_{\mathrm{s}} \left( \frac{I_{\mathrm{s,rms}}}{J_{\mathrm{m}}} \right). \label{eq:app_KuAw}
\end{equation}

Rearranging Eq.~\ref{eq:app_faraday_p} and Eq. \ref{eq:app_faraday_s} to solve for $N_{\mathrm{p}}$ and $N_{\mathrm{s}}$, and substituting them into Eq.~\ref{eq:app_KuAw} gives:
\begin{equation}
    K_{\mathrm{u}} A_{\mathrm{w}} = \frac{V_{\mathrm{p,rms}}}{K_{\mathrm{f}} f A_{\mathrm{c}} B_{\mathrm{m}}} \left( \frac{I_{\mathrm{p,rms}}}{J_{\mathrm{m}}} \right) + \frac{V_{\mathrm{s,rms}}}{K_{\mathrm{f}} f A_{\mathrm{c}} B_{\mathrm{m}}} \left( \frac{I_{\mathrm{s,rms}}}{J_{\mathrm{m}}} \right). \label{eq:app_KuAw_sub}
\end{equation}
Defining the input apparent power as $P_{\mathrm{in}} = V_{\mathrm{p,rms}} I_{\mathrm{p,rms}}$, the output apparent power as $P_{\mathrm{o}} = V_{\mathrm{s,rms}} I_{\mathrm{s,rms}}$, and the total apparent power handling capability of the transformer as $P_{\mathrm{t}} = P_{\mathrm{in}} + P_{\mathrm{o}}$ (where $P_{\mathrm{t}} \approx 2P_{\mathrm{o}}$ for a high-efficiency isolation stage), Eq.~\ref{eq:app_KuAw_sub} simplifies to:
\begin{equation}
    K_{\mathrm{u}} A_{\mathrm{w}} = \frac{P_{\mathrm{in}} + P_{\mathrm{o}}}{K_{\mathrm{f}} f A_{\mathrm{c}} B_{\mathrm{m}} J_{\mathrm{m}}} = \frac{P_{\mathrm{t}}}{K_{\mathrm{f}} f A_{\mathrm{c}} B_{\mathrm{m}} J_{\mathrm{m}}}. \label{eq:app_Pt}
\end{equation}
Finally, multiplying both sides by $A_{\mathrm{c}}$ and dividing by $K_{\mathrm{u}}$ establishes the fundamental $AP$ formulation:
\begin{equation}
    AP = A_{\mathrm{c}} \cdot A_{\mathrm{w}} = \frac{P_{\mathrm{t}}}{K_{\mathrm{f}} K_{\mathrm{u}} f B_{\mathrm{m}} J_{\mathrm{m}}}. \label{eq:app_AP_final}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{Relationship Between the $AP$ and Transformer Volume}
To establish how the physical three-dimensional volume of the transformer ($Vol_{\mathrm{trans}}$) scales with the Area Product ($AP$), dimensional analysis is applied using a characteristic linear dimension $l$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Volume Dimensionality:} The three-dimensional volume of a geometrically similar transformer core and winding assembly is proportional to the cube of its characteristic linear dimension $l$:
    \begin{equation}
        Vol_{\mathrm{trans}} = K_1 \cdot l^3, \label{eq:app_vol_l3}
    \end{equation}
    where $K_1$ is a dimensionless constant relating physical volume to the characteristic length $l$.
    
    \item \textbf{Area-Product Dimensionality:} Because both the window area $A_{\mathrm{w}}$ and the core cross-sectional area $A_{\mathrm{c}}$ have dimensions of length squared ($l^2$), their product ($AP$) is proportional to the fourth power of the characteristic linear dimension:
    \begin{equation}
        AP = A_{\mathrm{w}} \cdot A_{\mathrm{c}} = K_2 \cdot l^4, \label{eq:app_ap_l4}
    \end{equation}
    where $K_2$ is a dimensionless proportionality constant relating the area product to $l^4$.
\end{enumerate}

Solving Eq.~\ref{eq:app_ap_l4} for the characteristic linear dimension $l$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    l = \left( \frac{AP}{K_2} \right)^{\frac{1}{4}}. \label{eq:app_l_scale}
\end{equation}
Cubing both sides of Eq.~\ref{eq:app_l_scale} gives:
\begin{equation}
    l^3 = \left[ \left( \frac{AP}{K_2} \right)^{\frac{1}{4}} \right]^3 = \left( \frac{AP}{K_2} \right)^{\frac{3}{4}}. \label{eq:app_l3_scale}
\end{equation}
Substituting Eq.~\ref{eq:app_l3_scale} into the volume expression in Eq.~\ref{eq:app_vol_l3} establishes the fundamental volume scaling relationship:
\begin{equation}
    Vol_{\mathrm{trans}} = K_1 \left( \frac{AP}{K_2} \right)^{\frac{3}{4}} = \frac{K_1}{K_2^{0.75}} \cdot AP^{0.75} = K_{\mathrm{vol}} \cdot AP^{\frac{3}{4}}, \label{eq:app_vol_ap}
\end{equation}
where $K_{\mathrm{vol}} = K_1 / K_2^{0.75}$ is a dimensionless constant determined exclusively by the geometric shape and aspect ratio of the core. Substituting Eq.~\ref{eq:app_AP_final} into Eq.~\ref{eq:app_vol_ap} yields the complete analytical volume equation:
\begin{equation}
    Vol_{\mathrm{trans}} = K_{\mathrm{vol}} \left( \frac{P_{\mathrm{t}}}{K_{\mathrm{f}} K_{\mathrm{u}} f B_{\mathrm{m}} J_{\mathrm{m}}} \right)^{\frac{3}{4}}. \label{eq:app_vol_full}
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:app_vol_full} proves that for a fixed operating frequency and electromagnetic loading ($B_{\mathrm{m}}, J_{\mathrm{m}}, K_{\mathrm{u}}$), the physical volume of a transformer scales with the three-quarters power ($0.75$) of its apparent power rating ($Vol_{\mathrm{trans}} \propto P_{\mathrm{t}}^{3/4} \propto AP^{3/4}$), while any single linear dimension scales with the one-quarter power ($l \propto AP^{1/4}$).
